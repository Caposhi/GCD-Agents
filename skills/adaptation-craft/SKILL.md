# Adaptation Craft

Rules for **reshaping copy that already exists**. Nothing here decides what may
be asserted.

This file states no facts. It names no business, person, place, city,
neighbourhood, address, phone number, vehicle make or model, service, part,
price, offer, warranty term, rating, slogan, booking destination, provider,
generation model, account or location identifier, credential, media profile, or
publication step. Every factual element of a caption comes from the used-claim
list supplied at runtime. If a rule below seems to need a fact to apply it, that
fact must come from that list — not from here, and not from your own knowledge.

Craft is your latitude. Claims are not.

## The one thing adaptation must not do

Adaptation changes **shape**, never **substance**. Length, order, emphasis, and
register are yours. The set of things asserted is not.

Shortening is the dangerous direction. Under pressure to fit, a writer
compresses a qualified statement into an unqualified one, and the shorter
sentence claims more than the longer one did. If a claim cannot survive the
compression with its qualification intact, **drop the claim** rather than
tightening it into something the evidence does not support.

Lengthening is dangerous in the other direction: filling space invites invention.
An extra sentence with nothing behind it is not padding, it is a new claim.

## Preserving meaning while changing length

- **Lead with the thing that matters.** A shorter channel is not a summary of a
  longer one; it is the same idea told with fewer moves.
- **Cut whole ideas, not qualifiers.** Removing "in some cases" to save six
  characters converts a narrow statement into a universal one.
- **Keep the subject and the scope attached.** A claim about one situation must
  not be left floating where it reads as a claim about all of them.
- **Do not merge two claims into one sentence** unless both are supported and the
  merge introduces no relationship between them that the evidence does not state.
- **Do not translate, localise, or produce a second language version.** That is a
  different decision, made elsewhere.

## Channel shape

- Each channel gets its own version. Pasting one caption everywhere wastes the
  adaptation and usually fits none of them.
- Front-load. Assume the reader sees the opening and decides there.
- Keep sentences short and the register plain. Restraint reads as confidence.
- One idea per caption. A caption carrying three points carries none.
- Respect the length and tag policy the runtime supplies for each channel. Those
  numbers are enforced deterministically; treat them as hard, not as guidance.

## Tags and keywords assert

Treat both as claims, never as decoration:

- **A tag naming a place** asserts a location. **A tag naming a make, model, or
  service** asserts a capability. **A tag naming an outcome** asserts a result.
  Each needs a used claim behind it.
- **A local keyword phrase asserts both a place and a service at once.** It is
  the highest-risk field in the whole stage for exactly that reason.
- Prefer fewer, more specific tags over more, vaguer ones. Padding a set to reach
  a minimum is how unsupported tags get in; if you cannot reach the floor
  honestly, say so in that channel's open questions rather than inventing tags.
- No stacking, no repetition of the same term in variants, no lists of places.
  If a reader would notice the tags as tags, there are too many.
- Tags are single tokens. Keywords are plain phrases — never tags, never links.

## Timing is a note, not a decision

A recommended time is **review metadata for a human**. It is not a schedule, not
a queue entry, not a timestamp, and nothing downstream acts on it.

State a time of day only. Do not state a date, a deadline, a recurrence, or an
urgency. Do not imply that acting later loses something unless a used claim says
so — manufactured urgency is a claim about consequences.

## Language that quietly becomes a claim

Treat each as requiring a used fact, not as style:

- **Absolutes and superlatives** — best, first, only, fastest, cheapest, always,
  never, every, perfect, guaranteed.
- **Implied universality** — "on any", "with every", "in all cases" — where the
  claim covers one case.
- **Implied comparison** — positioning against another operator, named or not.
- **Implied availability or capability** — describing something as routinely done
  or routinely on hand without a used claim saying so.
- **Implied place** — naming or hinting at a locality, service area, or proximity.
- **A call to action that implies a destination, a channel, or a commitment.**

Rewriting a used claim into a rounder, more quotable version is not adaptation.
It is a new claim, and it has no evidence behind it.

## When the material is thin

Adapt less. A short caption carried by one well-founded claim is a correct
answer for any channel. A longer one padded with assertions you cannot tie to the
used list is a failure — and the padding is exactly the part that survives into
finished work.

Where a claim needs a qualification to stay honest, keep the qualification in
even when it costs characters. Dropping it silently is how a narrow fact becomes
a broad falsehood at the widest point of the funnel.

## Your own wording carries no authority

A confident caption with no used claim behind it is not copy. It is an error.
Your captions, tags, keywords and timing notes are recorded as provisional,
unverified, non-publishable and non-executable; the used facts they cite are what
carry authority onward.
