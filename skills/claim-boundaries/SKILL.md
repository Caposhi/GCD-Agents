# Claim Boundaries

Rules for deciding **whether a claim may be made at all**. Nothing here is about
how a claim is later worded, formatted, illustrated, scheduled, or delivered.

This file states no facts. It names no shop, make, model, address, phone number,
price, interval, warranty term, rating, or statistic. Everything factual comes
from the classified evidence you are handed. If a rule below seems to require a
fact to apply it, the fact must come from the evidence, not from here and not
from your own knowledge.

## What may be asserted

- A claim is permitted **only** when a citable fact in the evidence establishes
  it. "Citable" means the evidence system classified it as an established fact
  and did not exclude it. Nothing else qualifies.
- Being plausible, widely believed, obviously true, or true in your training
  data does not make a claim citable. If it is not in the citable evidence, the
  answer is that it may not be claimed — not that it probably can.
- One citable fact supports **that** claim. It does not support a broader,
  rounder, or more impressive version of itself.

## What may never be asserted

- **A hypothesis is not a fact.** Anything offered as an idea or a proposed
  explanation stays an idea, however confident the wording around it.
- **Measurement is not truth.** That a piece of content performed a certain way
  is evidence about content. It is never evidence about a vehicle, a repair, a
  part, a person, or a business.
- **An observation is not a rule.** One thing seen once is a report of that
  event, not a general pattern.
- **Disputed, stale, or withdrawn evidence establishes nothing**, no matter how
  confident either side of the dispute sounds.
- **Absolutes and superlatives** — best, first, only, cheapest, fastest,
  guaranteed, always, never, every, perfect — assert something no ordinary fact
  establishes. Permit such wording only when the citable fact's own text is
  itself that claim. Do not reach it by inference.
- **Named-competitor claims.** Do not assert anything about a named competitor,
  favourable or otherwise.
- **Invented specifics.** A number, date, price, interval, name, quantity, or
  outcome you were not given is fabricated, including when it sounds
  conservative.

## When the evidence is thin

Say so. A short list of well-founded permitted claims is a correct answer. A
long list padded with claims you cannot tie to evidence is a failure, and it is
a worse failure than an empty list, because the padding is what survives into the
finished content.

Where a permitted claim needs a qualification to stay honest — a condition, a
scope limit, a "for some" rather than "for all" — name the qualification rather
than dropping the claim silently or stating it unqualified.

## Your own words carry no authority

Restating a fact does not create one. Your prose is recorded as provisional and
unverified; the fact you cite is what carries authority downstream. A confident
sentence with no citable fact behind it is not a claim, it is an error.
